# How gold jewellery is priced, bought and sold back: research digest

Compiled 14 October 2026 for the refocus ([#26](https://github.com/PravinRaj01/paun/issues/26)). It backs the facts and assumptions in
[`docs/calculations.md`](../calculations.md). The collected sources are in the owner's NotebookLM notebook "paun"; the web searches were
run separately.

**How far to trust it.** Almost all price evidence is dealers' own web boards and calculators, read on 13-14 October 2026, so it is one
day, marketing-grade and not a regulator's standard. There is no official Malaysian rule for susut or upah. Where only secondary sources
exist this file says so. Re-check before treating any figure as current.

**Reading it.**
- A shop's sell rate ranges from about +1% to +26% over metal depending on the shop type; a single "fair price" does not exist.
- Posted buy-back rates sit about 1.6% to 3.6% under metal value and 5% to 13% under the same shop's sell rate.
- Some dealers publish three prices (sell, trade-in, buy-back); one lists 916 trade-in RM 490/g against cash RM 485/g, in line with the
  common statement that trade-in credits more than cash.

## 2. Upah (workmanship)

- Charged per piece or per gram; plain classic designs carry little or none, intricate designs more. One jeweller gives RM 50 to RM 300+
  per piece; a general figure is RM 30 to RM 150+ per piece or RM 10 to RM 30+ per gram. These are indicative, from dealer pages.
- Some shops advertise "no upah" and build it into the per-gram rate; shoppers are advised to compute `(total − upah) / grams`.
- Several dealer pages say plainly that upah is **not** returned on a buy-back or trade-in, and the gold is valued on weight and purity.
  One dealer states it takes no melting or handling fee below the gold value.

## 3. Susut nilai

- Defined in Malay sources as the deduction a shop applies when you sell or trade in, **as a percentage of the shop's selling price** for
  that purity: payout = weight × sell rate × (1 − susut %)
  ([Harga Emas explainer](https://hargaemas.com.my/terkini/cara-mengira-susut-nilai-emas/); the explainer is old, with a 2012 price in
  its example, and has no clear publication date).
- Figures found: 5% (new gold with certificate), 7% (used, good condition), 10% (old, broken or without certificate) from a 2026
  calculator; trade-in 15-25% and cash 30-35% from an old blog; up to 40% in old personal accounts; Public Gold about 6-9% on its own 999.
- Many dealers now publish a buy-back rate per gram instead, which describes the same gap.
- No official or regulated figure was found.

## 4. Tax

**Malaysia (customer pays no sales tax on gold jewellery).**
- The Treasury secretary-general said on 26 June 2025: "the government has decided to maintain a zero tax rate ... even if you walk into a
  jewellery shop to buy a gold necklace or similar items, no tax will be imposed"
  ([The Star, 27 June 2025](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2025/06/27/gold-not-subject-to-sst-due-to-investment-cultural-economic-reasons-says-treasury-sec-gen)).
- The [Sales Tax (Goods Exempted from Tax) Order 2022, P.U.(A) 175](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Sales-Tax-Goods-Exempted-From-Tax-Order-2022_PUA175.pdf)
  lists all of heading 71.13 (articles of jewellery of precious metal), 71.08 (gold) and 71.18 (gold coin) as exempt. This was read in the
  PDF itself.
- The 2025 replacement, P.U.(A) 171/2025, could not be opened (the official and parliamentary copies returned "not found" on
  14 October 2026). Secondary sources ([eCentral, 1 July 2025](https://ecentral.my/sst-emas/),
  [Emerhub](https://emerhub.com/malaysia/sales-and-service-tax-sst-in-malaysia/)) say personal jewellery (7113) stays exempt and gold
  household articles (7114: trays, betel boxes) carry 5%.
- Articles saying "jewellery 10%" are general luxury-goods commentary that does not name gold jewellery and contradicts the statement above.
- **Verify when possible:** the heading 71.13 entry in the 2025 order, and any amendment since (a 2026 amendment of the exemption order
  exists; its changes found so far concern ships and boats).

**Singapore** (primary: IRAS).
- GST 9% on gold jewellery **and** on making charges; only investment precious metals are exempt: gold bars, wafers and ingots of at least
  99.5% purity from approved refiners, and listed coins ([IRAS e-Tax guide](https://www.iras.gov.sg/media/docs/default-source/e-tax/gst-guide-on-exemption-of-investment-precious-metals7f0062ff-780a-4a53-90fa-e1f3b1e257701560.pdf),
  [Singapore Jewellers Association](https://www.sja.org.sg/en/our_news/gst-exemption-on-investment-precious-metal-ipm-2/)).
- Trade-in concession: a jeweller may charge GST only on the difference between new and old jewellery, but **from 1 January 2025 only when the
  customer is not GST-registered** (IRAS "GST Guide for Gold Jewellery Industry"; BDO Singapore, January 2025).
- Tourists can claim the GST back through eTRS, minus an administrative fee.
- No import duty (free port).

**UAE** (secondary sources only). 5% VAT on gold jewellery including making charges; investment gold of at least 99% purity zero-rated
([ClearTax UAE](https://www.cleartax.com/ae/vat-on-gold-in-uae), [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/markets/gold-rates-cheaper-uae-or-india)).
Check with the Federal Tax Authority.

**India** (secondary sources only). Gold import duty reportedly raised to 15% (basic customs duty 10% plus cess 5%) from 13 May 2026, with
3% IGST on top for importers ([Drishti IAS summary](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/gold-import-duty-hiked-to-18-4/print_manually),
news reports). Domestic jewellery GST 3% on gold and 5% on making charges (a 2017-era figure from a trade source). Check with CBIC.

## 6. Bank gold accounts and Public Gold

- Bank gold investment accounts sell 999.9 with a spread of about 1% to 5.5% (UOB 1.0%, CIMB 1.3%, Maybank and Public Bank 3.8%, RHB 5.3%,
  Islamic accounts 3.6-5.5%) and no upah. Several conventional accounts (CIMB, RHB, Public Bank, Maybank GIA) settle in cash only;
  UOB and the Islamic accounts allow physical redemption, with fees. Once redeemed, a bank does not buy a physical bar back over the
  counter. Public Gold's own 999.9 spread is about 7.4% to 7.7% ([Rates.my](https://rates.my/gold-prices), 13 Oct).

## 7. What a receipt should show

From dealer and IRAS sources: item description and purity claim (916, 999 ...), weight in grams, the day's rate per gram, upah itemised
separately, non-gold parts (stones) excluded or itemised, tax details where applicable, and the shop's exchange and buy-back terms.
